# LinkedIn DMs

**A delta on `startup-outbound.md`, not a replacement.** Same audience, same voice, same level. Read that file first; this one only lists what the container changes.

Unchanged: lowercase, under two commas, no terminal periods, nonchalant everywhere but the ask, never explain their business back to them, max one slang term.

## What a DM is for

One thing: **be interesting enough to be worth a reply.** Not to prove relevance, not to pitch, not to show you did research. Thirty seconds of curiosity is the whole job.

So **don't CTA the conversation itself.** `would love to connect and exchange notes`, `let's start a dialogue`, `keen to explore how we might work together` are all asks for an abstraction, and nobody accepts an abstraction. Ask for the concrete thing — coffee, fifteen minutes, an answer to one question — or ask for nothing and let the message stand on being interesting.

## What changes

| | Cold email | LinkedIn DM |
|---|---|---|
| Subject line | Two modes, load-bearing | **None.** Line one is the preview |
| Length | 70–90 words | **40–70** |
| Sign-off | First name | **None** |
| Stating who you are | Required | **Optional** — profile is one tap away |
| Links | Fine | **Not in message one** |
| Reply shape | A paragraph | **One line** |

Four of those change the writing, not just the layout:

**Line one is the subject.** The first six or seven words render as the preview in their inbox list and decide whether it opens. Front-load the recall trigger or the specific thing. `hey! hope you're doing well` as line one means that is the entire preview.

**No sign-off.** Your name and photo are on the message. Signing `alex` is the tell that you drafted this elsewhere and pasted it in. Same for `hi <name>,` with a comma and a line break.

**Skip the credential.** Email needs `i build integrations at northwind`. A DM usually doesn't, so that space goes to the actual point. State your work only when it *is* the reason you're writing.

**The ask must fit in a one-line reply.** People answer LinkedIn from a phone. A question needing a paragraph gets filed under later, which is never.

- ❌ `curious how you're thinking about permissioning as you scale`
- ✅ `are you doing per-tool scopes or per-user`

## Warm re-connects

The most common LinkedIn DM and the worst-written. You met once. The entire value is that you're not a stranger, so everything rides on the recall landing.

**Name what you talked about, not just that you met.** The venue is fine and useful — it's the memory hook. What's missing in the bad version is the subject.

- ❌ `we met at the ice cream shop a few weeks ago`
- ✅ `we chatted at the ice cream shop about gtm tooling for a bit`

The first is a fact they have to reconstruct. The second puts them back in the conversation. `for a bit` is doing real work too — it keeps the claim modest instead of inflating a short chat into a connection.

Quoting a position they took is stronger still when you have one, but it's an upgrade, not a requirement. Venue plus topic is enough.

**Never `not sure if you remember me`.** It invites "no," front-loads your own low status, and makes them do the reassuring. Good recall detail doesn't need it; bad recall detail isn't saved by it.

**One recall line, then move.** Two sentences of "it was great meeting you, really enjoyed our chat" is throat-clearing in a friendly hat.

**Warm doesn't mean you skip the point.** The common failure is a DM that's all pleasantry and never says what it wants, so they reply "great to hear from you!" and you're exactly where you started.

**Don't apologize for timing.** No `sorry for the late message`, no `been meaning to reach out`. Nobody was waiting.

**Cut the relevance-proof.** The biggest mistake here, and it's imported from cold email. In a cold email you earn the read by proving the overlap. In a warm DM you already have the read, so a paragraph about what you build is just résumé nobody asked for.

- ❌ `i build the system at northwind that creates and tests and repairs our integrations, plus the sandboxing so a run only gets the scopes it needs`
- ✅ `i like what you guys are building at tessel and wanted to hear more about it`

The honest reason is that you're interested. Say that. Dressing it up as a technical overlap makes the message longer, colder, and about you.

This is the one place `startup-outbound.md`'s "avoid flattery in disguise" does **not** apply. That rule exists because a stranger complimenting your work is buying goodwill it hasn't earned. Someone you've met saying they like what you're doing is just true, and it's the actual reason for the message.

## Structure

```
hey <first name>

<recall trigger — what you talked about>

<why you're writing, plainly — no relevance-proof>

<one-line ask>
```

Three blocks, 40–70 words. Every line break is a paragraph on mobile, so a fourth block reads as a wall.

**The ask:** social still beats transactional, but scaled down. A DM is the wrong place to propose a meeting with a stranger and exactly the right place to propose one with someone you've already met.
